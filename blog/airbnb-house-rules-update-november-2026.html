Airbnb's New House Rules: What Changed on November 3 and What Hosts Need to Do Before November 20

Airbnb updated its house rules announcement on November 3, but the policy still starts November 20. Here's what changed, what didn't, the review timeline and how to appeal a rule.

By Marisa @ RevWhisperLast updated November 7, 2026

Airbnb's new house rules policy still applies to all reservations booked on or after November 20, 2026. On November 3, Airbnb updated its announcement: "Requests" are being relabeled "Additional rules", listing reviews run from November 20 through March 2027, and hosts get at least 20 days to update a flagged rule or appeal it once. The limits on checkout chores didn't change.

On October 20, Airbnb announced changes to house rules, including clearer standards for what guests can be required to do. Airbnb says guests "have told us for years that checkout chores and unexpected rules make them hesitant to book." Hosts pushed back. Airbnb says it has heard from many hosts, and that some are concerned about losing control over the rules they set for their homes.

On November 3, Airbnb updated its announcement. It said, "We also could have done a better job explaining why and how we're making these changes."

If you read about that update and assumed Airbnb backed down, it didn't. The policy still applies to all reservations booked on or after November 20, 2026. What changed is how Airbnb labels your rules, how it will review listings and how you can push back on a decision.

What did Airbnb change on November 3?

  • "Requests" are now "Additional rules." Hosts told Airbnb that calling additional rules "Requests" was confusing, so they'll be labeled "Additional rules" in the app over the coming weeks.
  • Reviews are phased in. Airbnb starts its initial reviews on November 20, and they continue through March 2027. After that, it will review listings on an ongoing basis.
  • You get time to respond. If a rule on your listing doesn't follow the policy, Airbnb will let you know by email and in the app. You'll have at least 20 days to update it, or to appeal and explain why your home needs it. The Listing editor will also give tips for adjusting rules that don't follow the policy.
  • Lock up at checkout is now a standard house rule. The checkout instructions section was removed from the Listing editor. You can re-add other checkout instructions, like turning things off, as Additional rules under "Before checkout."
  • House rules are now a quick reply. If you want to send guests a friendly reminder during their stay, your house rules are available as a pre-written quick reply that matches your listing.

What didn't change?

The limits on checkout chores are the same. Airbnb's own examples draw the line:

You can ask guests toYou can't ask guests to
DishesPut used dishes in the sinkWash and put away dishes
LaundryGather used towelsRemove sheets from the bed or start a load of laundry
TrashSeparate trash and recycling into designated indoor binsTake garbage off site
UtilitiesTurn off the air conditioning when they leaveAvoid using a listed amenity

Hosts also generally shouldn't impose fines or other penalties. You can let guests know about fines your local government or homeowners association could charge, at the bottom of your house rules. Airbnb doesn't collect or enforce those fines for you, and you can't charge guests a fee for skipping cleaning or checkout tasks. You can still leave ratings and reviews based on cleanliness.

There are 7 standard house rules to choose from: maximum overnight guests, quiet hours, events, pets, smoking (including vaping and e-cigarettes), commercial photography, and lock up at checkout, where you can add where to return keys.

House rules also have to be on your listing when the guest books. Airbnb says hosts can't add new house rules after booking, including by message, signs in the home or in person. For more on wording a checkout message that stays inside the rules, see Airbnb Checkout Instructions.

When does the new house rules policy start?

Bookings made before November 20 are covered by the previous policy. The house rules that were on your listing when you received a reservation still apply to that reservation.

How do you appeal an Airbnb house rule decision?

Airbnb says it considers the type of listing and other relevant factors, including local laws, and may grant exceptions based on your situation. Its own examples: asking guests to dispose of trash in an animal-proof container in bear country, or limiting shower time because of drought-related water restrictions.

If Airbnb flags a rule you believe your home needs, select the "Submit an appeal" link in the email Airbnb sent you, or tap the "Required action" card in the Listing editor. You can only submit an appeal once, so make it count.

  1. Explain your situation in detail. Not "guests should clean up after themselves," but what makes the rule necessary at your home. Airbnb's own sample statements include a co-op with a communal kitchen where everyone washes their own dishes, and bins that need to go out on collection day for stays of a month or more.
  2. Upload supporting photos or files, such as a local ordinance or homeowners association rule.
  3. Review it before you submit, since you only get one appeal.

If Airbnb doesn't approve the rule, it will email you a deadline to update or remove it.

What happens if you keep a rule Airbnb hasn't approved?

Guests may see a notice on your listing page. If a violation continues, Airbnb's policy says it can lead to a notice flagging the issue to guests, reduced listing visibility in search, or other action under its Terms of Service.

That matters more than it sounds. House rules are now displayed more prominently on your listing, and guests can also read them in their Trips tab and in reservation reminders from Airbnb. A guest comparing your listing to the one next to it will see that notice before they book. Anything that gives a guest a reason to pick another listing costs you clicks and bookings, and reduced visibility in search costs you more.

If a rule isn't worth an appeal, it's usually not worth keeping.

Can Airbnb hosts still use contracts and waivers?

Only in specific cases. Airbnb's examples include legal and insurance requirements, homeowners association rules, rental agreements for monthly stays of 28 nights or more, Airbnb Luxe listings, and coverage for optional amenities like golf carts.

Airbnb doesn't mention pool or hot tub waivers specifically. A contract can be allowed when your insurance provider or the law requires it, so check with them before you rely on a waiver. Airbnb does require hosts to disclose safety hazards such as a pool or a body of water, and to explain how to use features like hot tubs safely.

An allowed contract goes under Contracts in your listing settings, as one PDF per listing, and it's visible to guests, so remove personal information first. Airbnb doesn't help enforce your contracts or require guests to sign them; collecting the signature is up to you. You can't ask a guest to sign a contract that wasn't on your listing when they booked.

What should Airbnb hosts do before November 20?

  1. Open your house rules in the Listing editor and compare each Additional rule with Airbnb's examples above.
  2. Remove or rewrite anything a cleaner would normally do.
  3. For rules your home genuinely needs, gather your supporting documents now, before you get the email.
  4. If you use a contract or waiver, confirm it fits one of the allowed reasons and upload it under Contracts.
  5. Move any checkout instructions you still need into Additional rules under "Before checkout."

The policy applies to bookings made from November 20, so anything you fix before then is in place for every new guest.

Sources: Airbnb's Important changes to house rules (October 20, 2026, updated November 3, 2026), and the Help Center articles Reasonable house rules, Add house rules to your listing, Limiting host-imposed contracts and other requirements and Add a contract to your home listing.
