The 15.5% Fee Squeeze: How Airbnb's New Host Fee Changes Your Pricing Math

Most Airbnb hosts now pay a 15.5% host-only fee, taken from the payout. Here's how to recalibrate every rate so you stop losing money.

By RevWhisper TeamMarch 13, 2026Last updated October 27, 2026

Most Airbnb hosts now pay a 15.5% host-only fee, taken from the payout: hosts connected to a PMS moved on October 27, 2025, and most others on December 1, 2025. A $200 night that netted $194 under the 3% split fee now nets $169. To protect your margin, reprice on net revenue instead of gross ADR, usually by raising base rates 10 to 15% and rolling part of your cleaning fee into the nightly rate.

When did Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee start?

Hosts connected to a property management system moved to the 15.5% host-only fee on October 27, 2025, and most other hosts moved on December 1, 2025. In 2026, UK hosts moved on June 22, and Mexico's rate became 16%.

Under the old split-fee model, you paid 3% and guests paid their own service fee. Under the host-only fee, you pay 15.5%. That is a 12.5 percentage point increase in your cost per booking.

Most hosts do not know this happened, because the change is invisible in the Airbnb interface. They kept their old pricing strategy, so their net revenue per night dropped overnight. If you did not recalibrate every rate, you are losing money on every booking.

What does the 15.5% host-only fee cost you?

A $200 nightly rate under the split fee nets you $194. Under the host-only fee, you net $169. That is $25 less per night, or $750 less per month on a property booked 30 nights.

Left unadjusted, the fee change costs hosts $750 to $2,400 per property per month. A listing earning $60,000 a year loses $7,500 in net revenue if the host does not adjust for the 15.5% fee.

Why is your current ADR the wrong number now?

Average Daily Rate is what guests pay. Net ADR is what you keep after fees. Under the old model your $200 ADR netted $194; now it nets $169. If your pricing tool or spreadsheet calculates on gross ADR, you are optimizing the wrong number.

Every dynamic pricing decision, every comp set analysis and every promotional discount rests on a metric that no longer reflects your actual revenue. Gross ADR hides the real impact of the fee, which leads to underpricing decisions that compound over time. Hosts optimizing for gross ADR instead of net ADR leave an average of $18,200 a year on the table across a 3-property portfolio.

Common mistake: Keeping the base rates, seasonal floors and last-minute thresholds you set under the split fee. All three need to be recalculated on net ADR, not gross.

How does the host-only fee hit your cleaning fee?

Cleaning fees do not escape the 15.5% fee. Charge $150 to clean and you net $126.75, so your cleaning fee costs you $23.25 in platform fees. If your actual cleaning cost is $120, your margin is $6.75, not $30.

Many hosts set cleaning fees to cover cost, not to contribute to profit. That was already a mistake, and now it is a bigger one. High cleaning fees also suppress your search ranking and scare off short stays: the algorithm penalizes total price, and guests filter by it (here is more on what the algorithm sees when it ranks you). You are paying Airbnb to rank lower. Listings with cleaning fees over $125 see 22% fewer one- and two-night bookings and rank an average of 14 positions lower in search results.

How does Airbnb's new dynamic pricing work?

In its September 30, 2026 Fall Update, Airbnb added built-in dynamic pricing. Airbnb recommends a price range, and with one tap, nightly prices adjust within it based on daily demand. The rollout started in October 2026. It doesn't set minimum stays or gap night rules, and it doesn't account for the 15.5% fee in your floor, so set your minimum price with the fee in mind.

How should you reprice for the host-only fee?

Start with net revenue targets, not gross ADR:

  1. Calculate your cost per booking, including the 15.5% fee, your cleaning cost and your fixed costs per stay.
  2. Set a net RevPAR floor.
  3. Work backward to the gross rates that hit your net targets. In most cases this means raising base rates 10 to 15%.
  4. Lower your cleaning fee and roll that revenue into your nightly rates.

The algorithm rewards a lower total price, and you recapture margin through occupancy. Hosts who rebalance cleaning fees into nightly rates after a fee structure change see 18% higher occupancy and $1,340 more net revenue per property per month.

Pro tip: Run your comp set analysis on net revenue, not gross. Competitors still on the split-fee model have a 12.5 percentage point cost advantage that you have to price around.

Frequently asked questions

When did Airbnb's host fee go up to 15.5%?

Airbnb moved hosts connected to a PMS to the 15.5% host-only fee on October 27, 2025, and most other hosts on December 1, 2025. Before that, most hosts paid a 3% split fee.

How much does the 15.5% host-only fee cost on a $200 night?

You pay $31 instead of $6, so a $200 night nets $169 instead of $194. On a property booked 30 nights, that is $750 less per month.

Does the Airbnb host-only fee apply to cleaning fees?

Yes. A $150 cleaning fee nets you $126.75 under the 15.5% fee, so if cleaning costs you $120, your margin is only $6.75.

How much should I raise my Airbnb rates after switching to the host-only fee?

Work backward from your net revenue targets. In most cases that means raising base rates 10 to 15%.

Should I lower my cleaning fee under the host-only fee?

Usually, yes. Lowering the cleaning fee and rolling that revenue into nightly rates keeps your total price competitive in search while you recapture margin through occupancy.

What is the difference between gross ADR and net ADR?

Gross ADR is what guests pay per night; net ADR is what you keep after platform fees. Under the 15.5% fee, base rates, seasonal floors and discounts should all be set on net ADR.
