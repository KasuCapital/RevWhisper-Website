01 / Marathon, FL · 4 BR Waterfront

More homes in Marathon.
More nights booked anyway.

Sombrero Beach Home already had the reviews, the dock and the location. What it didn’t have was a busy fall. Between last year and this one, Marathon went from 91 comparable homes to 113. From May to October the home still earned 35% more than the same months last year, and in September, the slowest month for similar homes nearby, it was booked at more than twice their rate.

Who this is for: A single owner with one waterfront home, in a market adding competition.

02 / The Revamp

What used to be. What it is now.

Guests kept mentioning the same three things in reviews: manatees behind the house, the walk to Sombrero Beach, and how fast Matt answered. We moved those to the top of the listing and reordered the photos.

The first five photos. The aerial photo is still the cover, now brighter. A dusk shot of the pool over the canal replaces the daytime one in the second slot, and the primary bedroom takes the living room’s place in the first five.

Title · after
Dog-Friendly • Pool & Boat Dock • Sombrero Beach
Description · before
Welcome to your Sombrero Beach waterfront home in Marathon. This renovated 4BR/2BA retreat features a heated saltwater pool, private dock with canal access, outdoor dining and grill, 4 bikes, beach gear, and an inflatable SUP.
Description · after
“We were welcomed by a manatee in the channel when we arrived.” That’s what guests keep saying about 61 Sombrero, our dog-friendly single-level home at the end of a quiet Marathon canal, a five-minute walk from Sombrero Beach.
Why we changed it

Guests kept writing about the same things, so the rewrite leads with them. A guest’s manatee story now opens the description, and the title names the dog-friendly home, the pool and the dock. The bookings backed this up: 37% of its Airbnb stays brought a pet and 45% brought children. The old description was a list of features that many canal homes in Marathon could claim.

03 / What We Changed

What we changed, in plain terms.

The problem. Guests loved it: location comes up in 46 of its 50 reviews and Matt’s responsiveness in 44. But every fall it sat mostly empty, because more homes were going after the same guests.
  • Checked the setup first: the extra charges added to the price on each booking site, the owner’s $300 lowest nightly price, the seven-night minimum stay and the Sabbath rules.
  • Rewrote the listing around what guests already loved: a new title on dogs, the pool and the dock, and a description that opens with a guest’s manatee story.
  • Reordered the photos and captioned all 24, so the dusk pool and the seawall dining show first.
  • Set prices date by date for open weekdays and the quieter weeks between seasons, based on where the home showed up in Airbnb search for those dates, and priced the holidays months ahead.
